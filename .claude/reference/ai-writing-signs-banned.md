# Signs of AI Writing — Banned / Avoid (Guidance)

**Source:** [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)  
**Purpose:** Use as **negative guidance** for all generated text (articles, summaries, cover letters, posts, docs). Do NOT write like this.

---

## Content — Avoid

### Undue emphasis on significance / legacy / broader trends
- Do not use: *stands/serves as*, *is a testament/reminder*, *vital/significant/crucial/pivotal/key role/moment*, *underscores/highlights its importance*, *reflects broader*, *symbolizing its ongoing/enduring*, *contributing to the*, *setting the stage for*, *marking/shaping the*, *represents/marks a shift*, *key turning point*, *evolving landscape*, *focal point*, *indelible mark*, *deeply rooted*.
- Do not puff up importance or add "broader context" for mundane facts.
- Do not add hedging preambles ("though relatively unimportant...") then talk about importance anyway.

### Undue emphasis on notability / attribution / media
- Do not use: *independent coverage*, *local/regional/national media outlets*, *profiled in*, *written by a leading expert*, *active social media presence*.
- Do not list sources as proof of notability without saying what they actually said.
- Do not create "Media coverage" sections that just list outlets.

### Superficial analyses
- Do not use: *highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing...*, *ensuring...*, *reflecting/symbolizing...*, *contributing to...*, *cultivating/fostering...*, *encompassing...*, *valuable insights*, *align/resonate with*.
- Do not attach "-ing" phrases at sentence ends for fake depth.
- Do not add synthesis or unattributed opinions.

### Promotional / advertisement-like language
- Do not use: *boasts a*, *vibrant*, *rich* (figurative), *profound*, *enhancing its*, *showcasing*, *exemplifies*, *commitment to*, *natural beauty*, *nestled*, *in the heart of*, *groundbreaking*, *renowned*, *featuring*.
- Keep neutral tone; no press-release or commercial tone for people/companies.

### Vague attributions (weasel wording)
- Do not use: *Industry reports*, *Observers have cited*, *Experts argue*, *Some critics argue*, *several sources* (when few cited), *such as* (before long exhaustive lists).
- Do not attribute opinions to vague authority or exaggerate how many sources hold a view.

### Outline-like conclusions
- Do not use: *Despite its... faces several challenges...*, *Despite these challenges*, *Challenges and Legacy*, *Future Outlook*.
- Do not end with rigid "challenges + vague positive" or "future prospects" paragraphs.

### Leads treating titles as proper nouns
- Do not open with "X refers to..." when X is a concept or list title.

### Vague "see also" / broad terms
- Do not fill see-also or related sections with overly broad terms or link to non-existent concepts.

## Language and grammar — Avoid

### Overused "AI vocabulary"
- Do not overuse: *Additionally* (sentence start), *align with*, *crucial*, *delve*, *emphasizing*, *enduring*, *enhance*, *fostering*, *garner*, *highlight* (verb), *interplay*, *intricate/intricacies*, *key* (adjective), *landscape* (abstract), *pivotal*, *showcase*, *tapestry* (abstract), *testament*, *underscore* (verb), *valuable*, *vibrant*.
- One or two may appear by chance; clusters = banned style.

### Avoidance of simple "is/are"
- Do not replace *is* / *are* with: *serves as*, *stands as*, *marks*, *represents*, *boasts*, *features*, *offers*.
- Prefer "X is..." over "X serves as a...". Prefer "has" over "features" or "offers".

### Negative parallelisms
- Do not use: *Not only... but...*, *It is not just about..., it's...*, *not ..., but...*, *no ..., no ..., just...* in a formulaic way to seem balanced.
- **Explicitly banned (Dex):** the mirrored two-sentence pattern *The problem is not X. The problem is Y.* (same for *The issue is not... The issue is...*, *It is not X that matters. It is Y.*, and close variants). It reads as template AI rhetoric. Say what is missing or what actually fails in plain language instead (e.g. name the missing pieces, or say teams lack A, B, and C).
- **Russian:** the same ban applies to parallel negation/contrast pairs used for rhetorical balance (e.g. «Проблема не в X. Проблема в Y.» as a formula), not to ordinary one-off negation in context.

### Rule of three (formulaic)
- Do not stack "adjective, adjective, adjective" or "phrase, phrase, and phrase" for fake comprehensiveness.

### Elegant variation (repetition penalty)
- Do not replace the same concept with different synonyms in every mention (e.g. protagonist, key player, eponymous character) just to avoid repeating the word.

### False ranges
- Do not use "from X to Y" when X and Y are not endpoints of a real scale (e.g. "from problem-solving to artistic expression" as if one scale).

## Style — Avoid

### Title case in headings
- Do not capitalize all main words in section headings (use sentence case unless proper nouns).

### Overuse of boldface
- Do not bold every instance of a term or use "key takeaways" style bolding throughout.

### Inline-header vertical lists
- Do not use lists where each item is "**Bold label:** description" in a mechanical way.

### Emoji
- Do not put emoji in section headings or bullet points (especially in professional/encyclopedic text).

### Overuse of em dashes (—)
- **Banned in our system** for cover letters, summaries, application text (see CLAUDE.md). Use commas, periods, or separate sentences. Even elsewhere, use sparingly; not every clause needs an em dash.

### Unnecessary small tables
- Prefer prose where a small table adds no value.

### Curly quotation marks
- In code/wikitext contexts straight quotes are required; in prose, consistency matters (we prefer straight in generated text unless quoting a source that uses curly).

### Subject lines in body text
- Do not paste "Subject: ..." into article or comment body.

### Skipping heading levels
- Do not jump from level 1 to level 3; keep heading hierarchy consistent.

## Communication intended for the user — Avoid

### Collaborative / meta phrases
- Do not use in generated content: *I hope this helps*, *Of course!*, *Certainly!*, *You're absolutely right!*, *Would you like...*, *is there anything else*, *let me know*, *more detailed breakdown*, *here is a...*.
- Do not address the reader as if the text is a chat reply.

### Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers
- Do not use: *as of [date]*, *Up to my last training update*, *While specific details are limited...*, *not widely available/documented*, *based on available information*, *maintains a low profile* (as speculation).

### Placeholder / template text
- Do not leave [brackets], INSERT_SOURCE_URL, 2025-XX-XX, or Mad Libs-style blanks in final output.

## Summary for our skills

When writing any text (cover letters, resume summaries, LinkedIn posts, articles, docs):

1. **No em dashes (—)** in application/career text (already in CLAUDE.md).
2. **No AI vocabulary clusters** (pivotal, crucial, underscore, tapestry, delve, foster, showcase, etc.).
3. **No puffery** (testament, vital role, evolving landscape, commitment to, nestled, vibrant).
4. **No weasel attributions** (experts argue, several sources, industry reports) without real citations.
5. **No formulaic structures** (Despite X, ... faces challenges; Not only... but...; mirrored *The problem is not X. The problem is Y.*; rule of three).
6. **Simple copulatives:** prefer "is/are/has" over "serves as/boasts/features/offers".
7. **Neutral tone:** no promotional or ad-like language.
8. **No meta/collaborative phrasing** in the deliverable (I hope this helps, let me know, etc.).
